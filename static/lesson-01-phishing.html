🐱 CatPersonal security

Protect yourself first

I can't look after anyone if my own logins are wide open. Neither can you. So we start with you.

  1. Read the domain after the @.Every letter. thisistea.example and thisistae-app.example belong to two different owners.
  2. Notice the rush.A two-hour deadline is a feeling someone else put in you. Feelings aren't evidence.
  3. Never type a password after clicking an email link.Open the site yourself, from a bookmark or by typing the address.

Zero trust isn't paranoia. It's checking.

🐢 TurtleOrganizational security

Shield the whole shell

In all my years I've learned one thing: a single open door lets the tide into every room. When you guard your inbox, you guard your neighbours too. It works a little like a vaccine.

  1. Confirm the unusual, together.A wire transfer, a password reset, an urgent file: check it on another channel. A phone call costs one minute.
  2. Tell the others.If a strange email reached you, it reached your colleagues too. Report it to IT so we can warn everyone.
  3. Give the whole team a second lock.With multi-factor authentication, one stolen password is a scratch, not a crack in the shell.

A team we can trust is a team that looks out for each other.
