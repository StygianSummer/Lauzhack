## Introduction

> **Satellite file.** What to build for MVP 0 is defined only in [docs/mvp0-plan.md](mvp0-plan.md). This file is the storytelling pitch and ideas for later. If this file disagrees with the plan, **the plan wins**.

Cybersecurity has always been a big thing, but now even more, especially
after "the dark october", where we have seen organizations getting attacked
like we haven't seen before.

That is why it is essential that employees, especially ones working with
sensitive information and systems, are well educated when it comes to 
social based attacks.

This will not solve cybersecurity but it is a great step in the right
direction.

Furthermore, directors, CEOs, anyone in charge should be able to understand 
their attack vectors and how can they protect their organizations even if 
it's on a surface level.

That is why we created Thisistea, to help educate people in charge,
employees, elderly, in general anyone who is not part of the technicall
community and teach them about cyber literacy.

## What are we building?

Think Duolingo for cyber security literacy: short daily lessons on spotting
scams and protecting yourself and your organisation. When you feel ready, you
can ask for a harmless practice email in your real inbox, to check that the
habit holds outside the app.

## What do we teach?

We target cybersecurity from two different perspectives. We educate people
on their personal security, how to put good passwords, how to manage them,
what to trust, what should they not trust, we teach them to be digitally
responsible. That is the foundation of what we want to achieve. It doesn't
matter if you have an amazing architecture and the latest auth tools if
your employeers write their passwords on sticky notes next to their laptops
:).

The second thing we teach, that is built from that, is how to secure your
organization. We help them identify bad practies, and we help them
implement good ones. For example, when someone leaves the organisation,
their accounts and access should be removed on their last day, planned
together with HR, so that no forgotten account stays open. This is just an
example.

We also provide a list of free tools that can help someone take care of
their online security.

## How do we teach it? What is our differenciator?

Well, we have light theory, teached on a light fast-paced way. Kind of
duolingo, some trivial questions. 

Then we have a way of teaching more difficult and extensive concepts [to be
thought on how to do this].

And then, we have practice in real life. Inside the app every message is
suspicious, because "it's the scam app", so the context gives the answer
away. That is why, at the end of a lesson, the learner can **ask** for a
practice email: the button says plainly that one is coming. They don't know
when it arrives or what it looks like; that is the test. It comes from our
own plain address, uses fictional names, never asks for a password, and its
link goes straight to a short, blame-free reveal ("this was the practice
email you asked for, here is what gave it away"). We only record whether the
link was clicked or the email was reported, never anything typed. The result
then appears in the next lesson.

Example: after lesson 1 the learner presses "Yes, send me one". A minute
later an email arrives about a "problem with your account". If they click,
the reveal shows the clues they missed; if they report it from the app, they
get a reward. Later lessons can offer other kinds of practice (an SMS, a
message from a fictional colleague, someone pasting company data into an AI
chatbot), always inside the app or with the learner's explicit opt-in. The
goal is a calm habit of checking, not fear.

That is how we do it.

## Story telling

Spiders, dog, cat, sea turtle.

Each one teaches you something.
- Turtle: Teaches you defensive security, how to secure your organization
- Dog: Has many friends, so he gives you resources that can help you
- Cat: Very selfish, teaches you how to secure yourself, first you
- Spider: Internet-web, web jajajaja xd, he plays the scammer in practice
  scenarios and shows you how each trick works


## What do practice scenarios look like?
All of these use fictional names and `.example` addresses, and run inside
the app (or, only after the learner opts in, as one harmless practice email):
- A sender address very close to a real one, to train reading domains.
- Messages asking you to "verify" something.
- A message that claims to come from a colleague.
- A message that claims to come from us: even then, check through a channel
  you control.
